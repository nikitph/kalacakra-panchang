Pañcāṅga · the five limbs of time

A pañcāṅga names every moment by five measures of the Sun and Moon. The Hindu day begins at sunrise, not midnight, and takes its name from whichever limbs are present at that sunrise, even when they change an hour later. Drag across the day ribbon to move through the day and watch the sky-wheel turn.

कालचक्र Kālacakra · the wheel of the sky, seen from above the north pole

  1. १Nakṣatra ring: 27 lunar mansions of 13°20′, each split into 4 pādas. Red marks the Moon's mansion.
  2. २Rāśi ring: the 12 sidereal signs. Blue marks the Moon's sign.
  3. ३Yoga ring: the Sun's and Moon's longitudes added together, cut into 27. The green diamond is that sum.
  4. ४Tithi ring turns with the Sun. Each cell is 12° of the Moon's lead over the Sun, shaded by how lit the Moon is.
  5. ५Karaṇa ticks are half-tithis. Red ticks are Viṣṭi (Bhadrā), avoided for beginnings.
  6. ☾The centre shows the Moon as it looks now. The gold wedge from the centre spans the Moon's lead over the Sun.

    मास Māsa · the lunar month

    Each cell is one tithi; the dates below it are the civil days whose sunrise fell inside it. Tithis run 19 to 26 hours, so they drift against the solar day. A tithi that starts after one sunrise and ends before the next never names a day (kṣaya, dashed). One that spans two sunrises names two days (vṛddhi, gold).
